Living together works best when we look out for one another. This community-maintained English guide follows the sections of the household regulations and aims to explain them accurately and clearly.

> **About this guide:** The original household-regulations PDF remains the definitive reference for the regulations. This page is not an official regulation or a replacement for that document. **Community notes** offer informal advice, not permission to depart from the rules. Sven does not accept responsibility for decisions made on the basis of these notes.

> **Community note:** Our informal approach to everyday life together is to be reasonable, responsible, and considerate rather than treat every interaction as a formal event. That perspective does not change the written requirements below or guarantee that a breach will have no consequences.

**Shared areas** means the spaces we use together, including kitchens, living rooms, corridors, stairs, shared bathrooms and toilets, the waste room, and the shared garden. It includes shared outdoor space as well as shared indoor space. This term has the same meaning throughout the guide; references specifically to the garden or outdoor space have that narrower scope.

## 1. Access and safety

### 1.1 Access

Access to the residence is reserved for residents. Visitors must **register at the entrance** and may use shared areas only during the permitted hours.

### 1.2 Fire safety

#### Clear routes

Keep **emergency exits and escape routes clear**. Everyone needs to be able to get out safely.

#### Fire-safety equipment

Residents and co-owners must follow the building's fire-safety rules. **Do not alter fire-safety equipment.**

#### Doors

Keep the building's entrance doors **closed at all times**. Make sure they close behind you when you enter or leave.

> **Community note:** Lock the garden doors again when you leave.

#### Following the regulations

Every resident must follow the regulations. Breaches may lead to sanctions imposed by the property administrator (syndicus) or the general meeting of co-owners.

### 1.3 Keys and badges

Keep your keys and access badges safe. If you lose one, **request a replacement from building management**.

## 2. Living together

### 2.1 Respect and noise

Treat fellow residents with respect. **Noise nuisance is prohibited**, particularly between **22:00 and 07:00**. Being mindful of how sound travels helps everyone feel at home.

### 2.2 Shared areas

Everyone appreciates a clean kitchen. Leave shared areas **clean after use** so the next person can enjoy them too. Respect the permitted use hours for these spaces.

> **Community note:** Sometimes you may need to step away before finishing the dishes. If that happens, let the group chat know as a courtesy, then return to wash up and clean the space. A message helps others understand; it does not replace cleaning up after use.

### 2.3 Visitors

Visitors must **always be accompanied by their host**. They may not **stay overnight** in residents' rooms **without prior permission**.

### 2.4 Quiet hours

Keep rooms and shared areas quiet between **22:00 and 06:00**. Avoid loud conversations, including at the front door and on the street. Ask your visitors to respect neighbours' sleep too.

The regulations give 22:00-07:00 in section 2.1 and 22:00-06:00 here. The noise-nuisance restriction in section 2.1 also covers **06:00-07:00**.

#### Garden use

All students may use the shared outdoor space. Keep its use to a **minimum between 22:00 and 06:00**. Noise nuisance to residents and neighbours must be avoided at all times; the regulations state that each breach is subject to a sanction. This applies to visitors too.

**Electronically amplified music is prohibited in the shared garden at all times**, including music played through speakers.

### 2.5 Visits and gatherings

#### Guests in your room

A gathering with **more than two guests in your room** must be **reported to building management in advance**. This counts guests, not everyone present: a host with three guests crosses the stated threshold.

This is a notification requirement for room gatherings. The PDF separately requires permission for community activities; it does not define those activities by this guest threshold.

#### Parties

**Student parties and other parties are prohibited** in the building and shared outdoor space. Student Home is in a residential neighbourhood, so avoid causing noise nuisance or disturbing neighbours. The prohibition on amplified garden music also applies here.

#### Community activities

Community activities require **written permission from the landlord or student coach** and must not disturb residents or neighbours. The PDF does not specify when an ordinary visit becomes a community activity.

Apply **at least one week in advance**. Include:

- **Activity:** what you plan to organize.
- **Organizers:** who is responsible, with **at least two students** organizing it.
- **Cleaning and safety:** who will be responsible for each.
- **Location:** where it will take place.

Costs for **direct and indirect damage** may be charged to the students who organize the activity.

#### Barbecues

**Barbecues are not allowed** inside the residence or in its shared outdoor space.

### 2.6 Support for residents and neighbours

The regulations provide for a first point of contact, available **24 hours a day, seven days a week**, for residents and neighbours experiencing problems with living together. The telephone number is missing from the supplied regulations.

The **student prevention coach** is the second point of contact and can also receive complaints about the first point of contact.

## 3. Use of shared areas

### Personal belongings

Keep **shared areas free of personal belongings**.

### Lift

Use the lift responsibly and with respect for the equipment. **Report damage immediately to the property administrator (syndicus).**

### Smoking

Smoking is **prohibited** in all **shared areas**.

> **Community note:** If you smoke outside near the front entrance, please move away from the entrance and front facade so smoke does not drift through residents' windows. Those living at the front of the building really appreciate this courtesy.

## 4. Maintenance and cleanliness

### 4.1 Room care

You are responsible for **maintaining your room**. Report any damage or defects **immediately to building management** so they can be addressed.

### 4.2 Waste management

Building management provides a **separate waste-collection system following IVAGO guidelines**. Follow that system and put each type of waste in its designated container.

Residents are responsible for taking **full waste bags, glass, cardboard, and GFT (vegetable, fruit, and garden waste)** to the **waste room on the ground floor**. The student coach checks that these arrangements are followed.

Building management is responsible for ensuring that the collection frequency meets the residence's needs.

### 4.3 Cleaning of shared areas

Regular cleaning does not replace our responsibility to leave shared areas **clean after use**.

The regulations provide for **weekly cleaning** of shared kitchens, living rooms, toilets, bathrooms and shower rooms, the waste room, corridors, and stairs. Other shared areas are to be cleaned **at least once a month**.

## 5. Use of equipment

### 5.1 Furniture and appliances

Use furniture and appliances carefully, including items such as your bed, desk, and fridge. **Do not alter or move them without permission.**

### 5.2 Shared equipment

**Follow the specific instructions** for shared equipment, such as washing machines and microwaves, and use it without disturbing other residents.

> **Community note:** Be considerate of the next person waiting to use an appliance. Once you have finished, leave it ready for them.

### 5.3 Electricity and heating

The regulations **recommend** avoiding leaving electrical appliances on unnecessarily to save energy. Small everyday choices can help us use less.

## 6. Waste disposal

**Sort waste correctly** and place it in the designated containers.

**Do not leave bulky waste in shared areas without permission.**

## 7. Bicycle storage and parking

### Bicycle storage

Students must **use the internal bicycle storage** to keep public space around the residence clear. **Do not park bicycles on public space**, including against the building facade or on surrounding pavements.

Within the bicycle storage:

- Use it **only for bicycle storage**.
- Park neatly in the designated space and treat the storage equipment with respect.
- **Keep passageways clear** for other users.
- Do not store bicycles with missing or broken parts that make them unsafe or unusable.

### Removal and responsibility

Bicycles stored contrary to these rules, or left unused for a prolonged period, may be **removed at any time, without warning or compensation, at the owner's expense**.

The regulations state that residence management is not liable for damage to or theft of bicycles, or for damage caused by bicycle storage to people, vehicles, installations, or buildings.

### Reporting problems

Bicycle-storage users must report the following to the **owner of the residence**:

- Defects in the bicycle storage.
- Vandalism or theft of bicycles, equipment, or installations.
- Bicycles that are unsafe, unusable, or left unused for a prolonged period.

### Parking

Parking spaces may be used **only by authorized people and vehicles**.

## 8. Pets

**Pets are not allowed.**

## 9. Renovations and repairs

Work that causes noise may take place **only during these hours**:

| Day | Permitted hours |
| --- | --- |
| Weekdays | **08:00-18:00** |
| Saturday | **09:00-16:00** |

Management must plan this work so it **does not coincide with examination periods**.

## 10. Handling problems

### 10.1 Reporting incidents

For maintenance problems, incidents, or conflicts, **inform building management as soon as possible**.

### 10.2 Conflicts between residents

A residence mediator may help residents try to resolve a conflict.

## 11. Sanctions

### 11.1 Breaches and changes to the regulations

The **general meeting of co-owners may amend the regulations**.

Failure to follow the rules may lead to **warnings, fines, or, for repeated breaches, termination of the tenancy agreement**.

### 11.2 Warnings and sanctions

Breaches may lead to warnings and fines as determined by the general meeting of co-owners.

## 12. Prevention worker

The prevention worker visits regularly and works with students to encourage respectful use of the residence. Their duties include:

- Checking cleanliness in shared cooking areas, halls, sanitary facilities, and the entrance hall.
- Speaking to students when those areas are left dirty; the students must then **clean them properly**.
- Checking cleaning work carried out by the landlord's appointed cleaner.
- Acting as a contact between students and the landlord, in close consultation with students.

The PDF lists **overlast@stad.gent** as the prevention worker's contact address.

### Application of the regulations

The PDF states that the regulations are binding on **all co-owners, tenants, and visitors**. Its effective date is left as **[datum]** in the supplied document.

## 13. Breaches and findings

### Reports and investigation

Breaches may lead to sanctions after the landlord learns the facts, such as **who, where, when, and what happened**, from students, neighbours, or management.

Reports from third parties are followed up and investigated. Existing camera surveillance may be used to obtain additional information where appropriate. **All irregularities are recorded.**

### Categories of breaches

The regulations give the following categories and examples:

| Category | Examples from the regulations |
| --- | --- |
| **Major safety risks** | Misuse of fire and safety systems; use of unauthorized electrical appliances; smoking. |
| **Serious misconduct** | Theft; vandalism; use or dealing of prohibited substances; unwanted intimate behaviour. |
| **Antisocial behaviour affecting other students' peaceful enjoyment** | Smoking; noise nuisance; bullying; physical or verbal violence; unmanageable visitors. |
| **Administrative negligence** | Failure to report defects; keeping pets. |

## 14. Sanctions

### Measures and fines

The landlord may use measures to discourage, stop, and sanction a tenant's behaviour. These range from **a warning, a fine, or an alternative sanction to termination of the tenancy agreement**.

The PDF sets out these fine ranges:

| Category | Fine, excluding VAT |
| --- | --- |
| Major safety risks | **EUR 50-250** |
| Serious misconduct | **EUR 50-250** |
| Antisocial behaviour | **EUR 25-100** |
| Administrative negligence | **EUR 25-100** |

Sanctions take account of the **seriousness of the behaviour, any repeat offences, the personal file, and the length of residence**.

### Damage costs and records

Established damage attributed to a responsible person is recovered from that person through an **invoice with an itemized statement of costs**.

Breaches are recorded in the student's personal file and may be grounds for **refusing a renewed application for accommodation**.

### Hearing and decision

When a breach is established and a sanction follows, the person concerned must always have **the opportunity to be heard**. Section 14 assigns the sanction decision to the **landlord**.

Sections 1.2 and 11 also refer to the property administrator and general meeting of co-owners in connection with sanctions. This guide retains those separate statements from the PDF.

### Criminal offences

The PDF states that a breach which also constitutes a criminal offence is **always reported to the police**. If criminal proceedings follow, the landlord may join them as a civil party to seek compensation.
